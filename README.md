*A manifesto for what we are building at [Ve](https://ve.ai) — the intent company.*

---

In 1995, two researchers at Xerox PARC — Mark Weiser and John Seely Brown —
named the idea that would take thirty years to become possible. They called it
**calm technology**: *technology that informs but doesn't demand our focus or
attention.* It would sit at the edge of your awareness, move to the center only
when you needed it, and recede the moment you didn't.

It was the right idea at the wrong time. For three decades we couldn't build it,
because we couldn't predict well enough to stay silent most of the time and be
right when we spoke. So computing went the other way.

The loudest decade of computing is ending. We are building what Weiser saw.

---

## The world we want

A world where the system is already with you.

Where the things you need are already there before you ask. Where the things you
don't need are quiet. Where you can work for an hour and forget anything is even
watching — and then, the moment you reach for something, it's in your hand.

Not a tool. A presence.

Not louder. **Calmer.**

---

## What "calm" means

Calm is not silence. Calm is *the right thing, at the right moment, in the
smallest possible form.*

A clock on the wall is calm. It tells you the time when you glance at it. It never
interrupts. It never demands. It's there when you need it and invisible when you
don't.

Calm computing is a clock that thinks.

---

## The four promises

**We perceive — but only what you allow.**
You decide what we see. Screen, voice, keystrokes, context — each is yours to
grant or revoke. We tell you, always, when we are looking.

**We predict — continuously, in the periphery.**
Behind the surface, we are already thinking about what comes next. You don't see
it. You feel it — in how fast the right thing arrives.

**We surface — only when we are sure.**
If we're not certain, we say nothing. A bad suggestion costs more than no
suggestion. Confidence is the gate. Your sensitivity slider is the dial.

**We disappear — until you need us.**
No popups. No notifications. No "hey, try this." Just a tiny presence in the
corner of your eye, breathing softly when it's thinking, settling when it's calm.

---

## The line we will not cross

We will never surprise you.

No silent capture. No hidden listening. No "we'll improve our model from your
data" buried in a settings page. Every signal we sense, you turned on. Every
moment we capture, you can see. Every prediction we surface, you can dial down to
silence.

The contract is: **you give us permission, we give you calm.** Break that, and we
have no product.

---

## Why this is the moat

Every AI product in the market today is loud. Copilot interrupts. ChatGPT waits
for your prompt. Cursor-the-IDE asks you to summon. Notion AI lives in a slash
command. Even the smartest of them is *a thing you turn on, talk to, and turn
off.*

We are the first that is *just there*.

That is not a smaller product. That is a bigger one. Because attention is the
scarce resource of the next decade — and the company that respects it wins.

## One line to remember

> **Your last five minutes, available to your next five seconds.**

That's what calm computing really is. Not a chip. Not a prediction. Memory across
surfaces, surfacing as if you'd already thought of it.

You read an email with three tasks. You open your task list. Before you type a
single letter, the first task is already waiting. You didn't ask. You didn't
switch context. The world simply *remembered with you*.

That is the product.

## How we test ourselves

Eight principles, from Weiser and Brown's 1995 work, sharpened by Amber Case in
*Calm Technology* (2015). We live by them now:

1. Technology should require the smallest possible amount of attention.
2. Technology should inform and create calm.
3. Technology should make use of the periphery.
4. Technology should amplify the best of technology and the best of humanity.
5. Technology can communicate, but doesn't need to speak.
6. Technology should work even when it fails.
7. The right amount of technology is the minimum needed to solve the problem.
8. Technology should respect social norms.

If any answer is no, we don't ship it.

## What we are not

We are not a writing tool. Grammarly is a writing tool.

We are not an assistant. Copilot is an assistant.

We are not a chatbot. ChatGPT is a chatbot.

We are a **presence**. A quiet companion. Calm computing — that already knows,
mostly stays silent, and arrives just in time.

We are the first to choose calm.
